# V0.oomd-frozen-work: past oomd's limit the guard ends one thread's heavy work itself, frozen work first and never an agent, before oomd ends a whole thread

→ V0.thaw-cap-oomd (5), V0.load-sim, V0.job-limits, V0.oomd-guard, decision 0036 ((B), (F)).

(1) **What the host log shows.** V0.thaw-cap-oomd (5) listed three gaps of 27 Nov. Read again with oomd's journal beside the guard's log:
- *oomd and frozen work.* Between 11:16 and 11:30 oomd killed six thread scopes and a container under the agents' slice, each scope with its agent. The agents' swap was full from 11:15 (10.1 of its 10.1 GB) to the end. oomd ranks candidates by their pgscan since its last poll (decision 0036, "Who oomd may kill"), and at 11:29:12 that delta was 2 pages for the scope it took. That scope's work had been frozen from 11:10:52 to 11:23:38 and was frozen again 3 s before the kill, taking turns, at a stall of 95 %. So the pick was close to chance among scopes the full swap left nothing to reclaim from. Fyr cannot steer it: oomd reads `user.oomd_avoid` only on a cgroup root owns (or one with the monitored cgroup's owner), and a thread's scope is delegated to its owner.
- *The swap valve.* At 11:15:31, 11:20:15, 11:22:25, 11:26:13 and 11:28:15 `why=swap-full` set `memory.high=max` on an e2e job and a make check, as V0.job-limits does at 90 % of the swap cap. The `agents_mem=12288MB/12288MB` in those lines is the whole agents' use. The jobs themselves stayed under the limits they had: that thread peaked at 4.6 GB against its 6.1 GB, and the make check never went over its share. The valve cost nothing that hour.
- *The one heavy work that runs.* With two threads that had heavy work, the guard held one frozen, the most it may. The other's 7.5 GB smoke run was let run by V0.freeze-cycle-cap (thawed 3 times in the hour) at a 46 % stall and up into oomd's limit. Near the limit, V0.thaw-cap-oomd now lets it be frozen again. But with the swap full a freeze frees nothing: the frozen pages have nowhere to go. It only stops growth.

(2) **The simulation.** `incident-27nov` never filled the swap (its peak was 7.5 GB). A new parameter `SwapBase` is the swap the scenario's other agents already hold (6.7 GB at 11:08 on the host). (4) **The rule.** When the agents' unit stall (the figure oomd compares) has been at or over oomd's limit for `OomdEndAfter` (10 s; oomd kills after its 20 s), the guard kills one thread's heavy work:
- the frozen work that holds the most memory, because it makes no progress and ending it frees its swap too;
- else the running heavy work that holds the most, never the oldest (one always runs) and never a service.
The agent is never killed. Running undeclared work is moved out of `cli/` into `undeclared/` first (as a freeze does), so the `cgroup.kill` takes only the work. A job's admission goes back, so its rerun is admitted again. The clock then starts again, so a second kill comes only if the stall is still over the limit 10 s later. The guard logs `guard: ended undeclared work thread=… before oomd: over its limit for 12s cmd="…" frozen=true procs=… mem=…`, and the owner's guard history (desktop and phone, one list in `web/src/guard.ts`) says "Killed undeclared work of <thread>: memory stalled past oomd's limit, which would have ended the whole thread". The agent is told at its next turn (a Next notice, never a wake): "Fyr killed your heavy work (`…`): the agents' memory stalled past systemd-oomd's limit, and oomd would have ended this whole thread, you with it, within seconds. Its failure or exit 137 came from that, not from your change. Run it again when you need it: Fyr admits it once memory has recovered and it fits." Nothing appears on the owner's screen beyond those lines, so this never interrupts what the owner types. Without oomd's limit (macOS, no rule on the slice) the rule never acts. The owner chose to ship it on by default on 27 Nov. It amends decision 0036 (F), where only classes the owner marked rerunnable were killed.

(5) **Not changed.** The swap valve stays, because it cost nothing in the log or the table, and a limit held with no swap room livelocks its job. The cycle cap and the cap of frozen threads (the threads with heavy work less one) stay, since every change to them made other rows worse.
