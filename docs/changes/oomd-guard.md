# V0.oomd-guard: fyrd asks oomd to skip its own cgroup, warns near oomd's limit, and keeps a job frozen past 10 min while memory stalls critically

→ decision 0036 slice 0 ((B) until #221, (E2)), V0.151 (3), V0.164 section 7, V0.multi-freeze. (owner, 19 Nov: "What is the best way to manage this? What are we missing?")

(1) **The cause.** On 18 Nov at 15:30:18 systemd-oomd marked `fyr.service/server` (fyrd itself, 2.2 GB, its own pressure 0) for killing, because `fyr.service` was at 74.8 % memory pressure against its 70 % limit. The guard's last line before it was at 15:23:29 (`memory short: headroom=5% stall=ok … running=1`); it did not see the unit's stall that oomd read. From 18 to 19 Nov all six thaws for "10 min limit" happened while the host's and the agents' stall were both critical; on 19 Nov from 09:58 to 10:31 the agents' headroom was 0–1 % and the stall critical for 33 minutes, and each thaw let the job back into it.

(2) **oomd skips fyrd.** At start fyrd writes `user.oomd_omit=1` on `server/`. `server/` has the same owner as `fyr.service`, so oomd honours it on a pressure kill under that unit's rule. Best effort: a failure is logged (`cgroups: user.oomd_omit not set on server/ …`) and nothing else changes. It does not protect fyrd from a rule on a root-owned ancestor (`/`, `system.slice`) or from a swap kill; that is #221 (decision 0036 (B)).

(3) **Near oomd's limit.** fyrd reads the unit's `ManagedOOMMemoryPressure` and `ManagedOOMMemoryPressureLimit` with `systemctl show` once at start. Every read of the load also reads the unit's own `memory.pressure` full avg10, the figure oomd compares. When it is within 10 points of the limit, the log says, at most once a minute: `guard: near oomd's limit: unit_full=62% limit=70%: oomd kills under this unit after its duration`. `GET /api/load` adds `unitMemoryStall` and `oomdLimit` (absent without the cgroup tree or without a rule).

(4) **No timed thaw into a critical stall.** The 10 min limit of V0.151 (3) thaws a frozen job or frozen undeclared work only while neither the host's nor the agents' memory stall is critical. Otherwise it stays frozen, the log says `guard: keeping thread=… frozen past the 10m0s limit: memory stalls critically (…)` (once a minute), and the thread is told once: "Your heavy job stays frozen past 10 min: memory still stalls critically on this host, and thawing it now would make that worse. It resumes when the stall eases; Fyr tells you then." When the stall drops below critical it thaws (`reason="10 min limit, stall no longer critical"`). A freeze whose work ended or whose processes are gone still thaws at once. The pause notices now say "resume when memory has recovered for 30 s, or after 10 min unless memory still stalls critically then"; so does the strip under a paused thread's header (desktop and phone, one component).
