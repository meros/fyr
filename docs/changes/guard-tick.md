# V0.guard-tick: the resource guard's policy lives in internal/guard, and one pure Tick makes every decision of the memory guard's tick; nothing the guard decides changes

→ V0.guard-mechanism (4) item 1, decision 0029, decision 0032, decision 0036. Second slice of #458.

The policy was in three places: the admission rules and the budget controller in `internal/slots`, the admission that feeds them in `internal/admission`, and the freeze, thaw, wake, turn and kill rules in the engine (`freezeGuard`, `guarddecide.go`, `tunables.go`, `undeclared.go`, `thaw.go`, `rotate.go`, `rerun.go`), which kept their state in a dozen engine fields and called the host's controls between its decisions. This change moves the policy into `internal/guard` and changes no decision: the 17 scenarios of the load simulation (V0.load-sim) give the same table, and the guard log of every scenario is the same line for line (thread ids and wall times aside), before and after.

(1) **Admission.** `DecideWith`, `Decide`, `EASY`, `Cheap`, `Thrashing` and their shares, and the budget controller `AIMD`, move from `slots` to `guard`; `internal/admission` becomes `guard.Admission` (`guard.NewAdmission`). The resource names (`ResCPU`, `ResMem`, `ResHeadroom`, `Wire`) are `resource`'s, and a class's learned measures are `resource.Learned` (they were `cgroup.Learned`), so the policy reads them without importing the mechanism. `slots` keeps the queue and its backfill cap, and installs a policy through `slots.Admitter` (`pool.Install(adm)`); it no longer holds a rule. Admission stays a call the pool makes at a heavy command's gate, not a tick's decision: it must answer at once.

(2) **`guard.Tick(in, st) → (decisions, next)`.** The memory guard's tick is one function of its inputs (the signals, the trigger, the holders, the flags, the running threads, a read-only `View` of per-thread readings) and an explicit `guard.State` (what is frozen, the holds frozen once, the episodes of undeclared work, short since when, the wake cap and clock, the rerun kills, the memory notice's episode). It returns `Decision`s of these kinds: freeze, freeze undeclared, thaw, rotate, kill, flag now, notify, notify memory, record, log, raise, release, and the hand-offs the engine keeps (thawed, rerun wait, keep kills, clear gate). The engine (`engine/freezeguard.go`) only gathers the inputs, applies the decisions through its controls, and turns the notices into Inputs.

Where the old code needed the host's answer before it went on (whether a freeze took, how many processes a kill found, which thread the fast flag picked, the memory read after a freeze, the signals the memory notice reads after the freezes), the decision is an ask. Tick stops at the first ask it has no answer for; the caller applies the decisions so far, answers, and calls Tick again with the same state, and the deterministic Tick returns the same decisions first and goes on (`guard.Run`). So the controls are called in the same order and with the same readings as before. The `View` answers each question once per tick (the engine memoizes it), so every call of Tick in one tick reads the same values, as the one pass did.

The state is written back once, at the end of the tick, instead of at several points within it; nothing outside the tick's goroutine read it in between for a decision.

(3) **What is left** of V0.guard-mechanism (4) (done in V0.guard-finish, except the last sentence, which its (5) keeps):
- The budget step (`AIMD.Step`, with its file kept across a restart), the slots' idle sweep (`sweepSlots`), the heavier-than-predicted notice and the gate's hold while memory is short are still called by the engine; they are guard policy and can join Tick (the budget as a decision, its file as a hand-off).
- Items 2 to 4: the job-limit sizing in `cgroup/joblimit.go`, a process-table Mechanism of its own, and the rest of `cmd/fyrd/wire_guard.go`'s closures. The engine still calls the host's controls through `SlotDeps` and `UndeclaredDeps` rather than one `guard.Mechanism`.

(4) The file-cache finding of V0.guard-mechanism (5) stands: the new policy should take a signal that tells reclaim stalls of the agents' file cache from a shortage of anonymous memory. This change keeps the guard's behaviour as it is.
