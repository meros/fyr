# V0.freeze-charge: work the guard freezes keeps no limit below what it needs, and the reclaim after a freeze asks the cgroup its memory is charged to

→ amends V0.joblimit-slice (2), V0.guard-swap (3) and V0.guard-tidy (1); decision 0036 (C).

(1) **The cause.** On 19 Nov at 20:48 memory was short (agents at 12214 MB of 12288 MB) and the guard moved t_5428…'s work into `undeclared/` and froze it: `guard: job limit … cgroup=undeclared class="" high=73MB … why=unknown`, then `froze undeclared work … mem=11.6 GB`. For the next 6 minutes the agents stayed at 12.2 GB and their swap at 115 MB: the freeze's reclaim moved nothing. Two faults:
- A class not learned yet got the agents' free memory (at most half their budget) as its `memory.high`, once, at the move (V0.joblimit-slice). The guard moves work into `undeclared/` only when memory is short, so the free memory is near 0 there by construction: 73 MB. After the thaw every allocation of the work would be throttled down to it.
- The kernel leaves a page charged to the cgroup that first touched it; moving a process does not move its charge. The work grew in the thread's `cli/` and was moved at the freeze, so its 11.6 GB stayed charged to `cli/`, and `memory.reclaim` on `undeclared/` (V0.guard-swap) had nothing to take. V0.guard-tidy (3) saw the same charge from the other side (the parts read `mem=0.0 GB`).

(2) **No limit at the guard's move.** Undeclared work of a class not learned yet gets no `memory.high` (log: `high=max … why=unknown-frozen`). Any number taken at that moment is the smallest the free memory gets, and so the most likely to be below what the work needs; the freeze already bounds the work, and after the thaw the guard's next tick does. A limit there would also count only what the work allocates after the thaw, since its pages stay charged to `cli/`. A learned class keeps its learned limit, and the turns of V0.guard-tidy (5) are unchanged.

(3) **An unknown job is never limited below the prior.** A job of a class not learned yet that fyrd moves into `job/` gets the free memory as before (at most half the budget), but never less than the prior, 1/N of the agents' budget (N = the host's `slots`), as admission counts it and as V0.admission-memory sizes a class learned from under 3 runs (log: `why=prior`). A grant while memory is tight no longer hands the job a few MB.

(4) **The reclaim asks `cli/`.** After it reclaims the frozen cgroups, Fyr adds up the frozen processes' anonymous resident memory (`statm` resident less shared) and asks the thread's `cli/` for the part the frozen cgroups do not hold, never more than `cli/` holds beyond its own processes (the CLI and its MCP servers), and within the same swap room, with the same `swappiness=200`. The frozen pages are the coldest in `cli/`: nothing touches them while frozen, so the kernel's LRU takes them before the CLI's working set. Measured on this host (kernel 7.3, MGLRU on): a 300 MB child that grew in `cli/`, moved to `undeclared/` and frozen left `undeclared/` at 0 MB and `cli/` at 344 MB; the old reclaim moved 0 MB, the new one 299 MB in about 1 s, while a 40 MB "CLI" that rewrote its pages every 20 ms in `cli/` lost none of them to swap (0 MB). V0.guard-tidy (1) still holds: no reclaim at all while the unit's stall is within 10 points of oomd's limit, since a reclaim raises `cli/`'s pgscan and oomd, with `memory.oom.group=1` on the scope, would end the whole thread.

(5) **Not in this change.** In a rotation, a unit's pages charged to `undeclared/` itself (grown there after a thaw, before the units were made) are not asked for: a reclaim of `undeclared/` would take from the unit whose turn it is as well. Decision 0036 notes that decision 0032 measured no gain from `memory.reclaim`; the charge in `cli/` may be why.
